How much of the real x402 economy does CDP's catalog capture?

This is our best-verified finding to date, corroborated by two independent methods rather than one. Every other page on this site is built entirely from Coinbase CDP's Bazaar catalog — this page is about how much of the real economy that catalog actually sees.

CDP's own Bazaar catalog — the source for every other page on this site — captures roughly 3% of real x402 transactions and 9% of real dollar volume over the same 30-day window. More than 9 in 10 real x402 transactions, and more than 90 cents of every real dollar moved, never appear anywhere in the catalog this whole site is otherwise built from.

Two independent methods, not one

Method 1 — on-chain positive control
We queried Base RPC directly (eth_getTransactionCount) against all 40 of Coinbase's facilitator addresses and got 106,602,243 transactions sent, all-time. x402scan independently publishes an all-time count for the same facilitator of 106,581,020 — a difference of 0.02%. This is a positive control: it confirms x402scan's transaction counting reflects real on-chain activity we can independently reproduce, before we lean on any of its other numbers.
Method 2 — matched 30-day window, two sources
With x402scan's counting validated by Method 1, its own stated 30-day window (12,421,896 tx / $711,166) is compared directly against our own full sweep of CDP's catalog over the same kind of window (373,056 tx / $62,801). Both the transaction-count ratio (3.0%) and the dollar-volume ratio (8.8%) land in the same single-digit-percent range, which is why we call this converged rather than a one-off measurement.

What this does not establish — read before quoting this number

  • The "real" 30-day figure is itself heavily concentrated in one merchant. A single address (0xe9030014f5dae217d0a152f02a043567b16c1abf) accounts for 9,401,793 of the 12,421,896 real transactions — 75.7% of all activity — at $0.0169 per call, from only 1,137 unique buyers. That looks like one high-frequency, low-value integration, not broad organic adoption. We have not recomputed the coverage percentages with that merchant excluded; treat 3.0% / 8.8% as coverage of the economy as it currently exists, concentration and all, not evidence of thousands of routes each doing meaningful independent volume.
  • The refuted $52.6M all-time figure is not used anywhere in this calculation. Dividing that figure by any plausible 30-day rate implies roughly 74 months of history, and x402 is far younger than that — see methodology.
  • x402scan's 30-day figure is vendor-self-reported, from one paid call. Method 1 above validates that its transaction counting matches independently-observed on-chain activity; it does not independently re-verify the dollar figure line by line.
  • Our own $62,801 CDP-derived GMV carries the same upper-bound caveat as everywhere else on this site: cheapest listed price × CDP-reported call count, not observed revenue. See methodology.
  • This measures catalog completeness, not total addressable market. A low CDP coverage percentage says a lot of real x402 activity is invisible to anyone reading CDP's Bazaar catalog alone — the entire reason this project exists — not a judgment on how healthy or large the ecosystem is in absolute terms.
