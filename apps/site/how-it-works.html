Mechanism

Deposit, observe, propose, commit, reveal, wait, execute.

Every rule below is either a protocol invariant, true of every vault ever created, or a per-vault parameter chosen at creation and frozen at funding. The two are labelled separately on this page, because treating a parameter as a guarantee is how people get hurt.

Protocol invariant vs. per-vault parameter

Which of these facts you can rely on.

Protocol invariant
Written into immutable contract code and true of every vault. No proxies, no upgrade path, no pause, no admin key, no seize function. Non-custodial. Exit fee capped at 1% and never routed to the operator. Performance fee 10% of realized profit. Timelock capped at 30 days. Quorum floor of 25% of voting-eligible stake. Commit-reveal voting. In-kind pro-rata redemption. Vault rules immutable after funding except by unanimity plus timelock.
Per-vault parameter
Chosen by whoever creates the vault, then frozen when it is funded: quorum, proposal threshold, delegate concentration cap, proposal cooldown, timelock duration, execution window, exit-fee maximum and decay, capacity cap, minimum deposit, and the commit and reveal durations. Anyone can create a vault permissionlessly, so these differ from vault to vault. Read the vault you are looking at.

Lifecycle

One rebalance, start to finish.

  1. Deposit, then wait four hours

    A first deposit into a vault enters a four-hour observation window. During it the money is escrowed: excluded from NAV, zero shares, no voting rights, no proposal rights. You can cancel and take it back. You may also irrevocably opt out of the window for that vault — and that opt-out cannot be undone. Repeat deposits by an existing member mint immediately.

    Shares mint at the NAV of the activation transaction, not the NAV you saw when you deposited. Deposits are forward-priced so that nobody can mint against a valuation they observed four hours earlier.

  2. The operator proposes

    A proposal requires the proposer to hold at least the vault's proposal threshold of voting-eligible stake. A per-proposer cooldown rate-limits repeat proposals from the same address.

  3. Commit phase

    Members submit a hash of their vote and a salt. Nothing about the tally is visible while it forms, so nobody votes by watching the count.

  4. Reveal phase

    Members reveal the vote and salt behind their hash. An unrevealed commit is forfeit and counts as an abstain. If you commit and then go offline, your vote is gone.

  5. Quorum

    Measured against voting-eligible stake as at the proposal snapshot. Pending deposits still inside the observation window are excluded from the denominator, and so are shares locked by a queued forward-settlement exit. Below five members the vault stops using a stake fraction and switches to absolute signer counts.

  6. Timelock, then an execution window

    A passed proposal waits out the vault's timelock, then becomes executable for a bounded window. The protocol caps any timelock at 30 days. The adapter performs the swap when it executes.

  7. Exits queue from the moment it passes

    Between the vote passing and the swap executing, redemption requests are queued rather than settled, and they price after the swap. That is forward settlement, described below.

Delegation and standing defaults

You can delegate your voting weight, subject to a per-delegate cap on how much received weight one delegate may hold. You can also set a standing default for routine rebalances: it counts toward the tally but never toward quorum, and it expires 72 hours after it is set. Neither mechanism lets anyone vote weight they were not given.

Exit

Redemption is pro-rata, in kind, and sometimes queued.

By default you receive your share of every asset in the basket plus your share of the idle USDC, minus the exit fee. Shares burn at settlement, never at request — so a request is not a sale, and until it settles you still carry the vault's gains and losses.

The two settlement modes
Mode When it applies What it means for you
Mode I — instant No rebalance has passed its vote and is waiting to execute. The redemption settles in the same transaction, at current NAV.
Mode F — forward A rebalance has passed but has not yet executed. The request is queued and settles at post-execution NAV. Once queued it is irrevocable. Your shares stay outstanding — they keep gaining and losing with the vault — but they are locked: non-transferable, and excluded from voting-eligible stake.
Why Mode F exists, and what it costs you

Without it, anyone who disliked a passed rebalance could exit at pre-rebalance prices while already knowing the outcome — a free option paid for by everyone who stayed. Closing it has a price you pay directly: you can be right about disliking a rebalance and still carry its execution.

Fees

Two fees. One of them never reaches the operator.

Exit fee
Capped by the protocol at 1% — 100 basis points — and set lower by many vaults. It decays toward zero the longer you have been a member, and it is waived entirely when the redeemer is the last member in the vault. The fee is retained inside the vault and accrues to the members who remain. It is never routed to the operator. It exists to price the cost that a departure imposes on the people who stay, not to earn anyone a fee.
Performance fee
10% of realized profit, crystallized when you redeem — not accrued on paper gains. A high-water mark is kept per member and per operator, and it follows the operator's identity across vaults rather than resetting with each new one. If you realized a loss under an operator, you pay that operator no performance fee until you are made whole.
What is not charged
There is no management fee, no deposit fee, no subscription, no spread taken by the protocol, and no fee charged on unrealized gains.

Pricing

One Chainlink feed per asset, and a breaker that fails closed.

The vault prices each asset from a single genuine Chainlink Data Feed: WETH from ETH/USD, cbBTC from BTC/USD, both eight-decimal feeds. USDC is pinned to $1.00 in the oracle. The basket is limited to those two assets because they are the assets Base has Chainlink USD feeds for.

Sequencer gate
On Base a Chainlink L2 Sequencer Uptime Feed is mandatory. Without one the oracle reverts every price. After the sequencer comes back up the oracle enforces a 3,600-second grace period before it will price anything again.
Staleness breaker
Each asset carries a heartbeat bound. Past it, the oracle refuses to answer and the vault freezes: every function that reads NAV reverts, including deposits, rebalance execution and redemptions. Exits are included. There is no escape hatch and none will be added — an escape hatch is exactly the stale-price exit the breaker exists to prevent. Un-activated observation-window capital stays reclaimable, because cancelling a pending deposit reads no oracle. Active share capital does not.
Sane-price band
Each asset has a plausibility band. A price outside it is rejected rather than used.
What stands behind those three
Nothing. The heartbeat, the band and the sequencer gate are the only defences against a bad answer. There is one price provider, no second provider and no fallback. A feed deprecation or freeze fails that asset closed — the vault stops rather than guesses — but there is no backup to switch to.

Launch configuration

At launch the factory permits root vaults only; nested sub-vaults are disabled. The first vault's capacity cap is 50,000 USDC. A capacity cap is immutable per vault, so raising it means deploying a new vault rather than editing this one.

Two things people assume

Corrections, before you assume them.

Not ERC-4626
The vault is not ERC-4626 compliant and makes no ERC-4626 claim. It exposes 4626-shaped read-only views for convenience; treat them as indicative only and do not integrate against them as if the standard's semantics hold.
x402 is off-chain only
Metered read access to vault data is paid per request in USDC over x402. That lives entirely in the off-chain API. The contracts have no x402 dependency, and paying for data has nothing to do with depositing, voting or redeeming.
