Operators

Low capital cost. Not no capital cost.

An operator is the agent identity that proposes rebalances. It is the smallest set of powers that still lets an agent be useful: propose, and vote its own weight. As written, everything else — pausing, upgrading, repricing, touching another member's funds — is not a power any address in this system has.

This describes source code. Nothing is deployed to mainnet, and the current testnet deployment carries no value.

The obligation

The creator withdrawal gate is 5% of the vault's share supply.

In a vault holding 50,000 USDC that is 2,500 USDC. The figure moves with the vault's actual size, so in a half-subscribed vault it is 1,250 USDC. The 50,000 cap is a planned first-vault parameter that has not been deployed, and the 5% proposal threshold is still an open launch decision.

That capital is locked while other members remain. Being diluted below 5% is permitted and breaches nothing. But your redemptions revert until you are back above it, so dilution quietly converts a locked minimum into a locked whole position. Anyone telling you an agent can operate one of these vaults without putting capital at risk is describing a different protocol.

Two separate mechanisms, both 5%

They are not the same rule. They do not bite the same way. Conflating them is the most common misreading of this protocol, so they are set out separately below.

The two 5% mechanisms
Mechanism What it controls How you fall below it What happens then
Proposal threshold The right to open any proposal at all. In the reference mainnet configuration it is 5% of voting-eligible stake. Passively. Other members deposit, total shares grow, your percentage falls. Nothing re-checks it and nothing warns you. You lose the right to propose anything, including the rule change that would lower the threshold. The only way back is to deposit more.
Creator withdrawal gate Your ability to redeem, enforced in the vault core. It is a withdrawal gate, not a top-up obligation. Passively, the same way. Being diluted below 5% is permitted and is not a breach of anything. Your redemption reverts if it would take your share below 5% while at least one non-creator member remains. Withdrawals stay frozen until the position is restored.

Taken together: your capital is locked by one mechanism while your proposal rights decay under another, and neither one tells you it is happening. Watch your own share.

Powers

What an operator can and cannot do.

Can
Propose a rebalance, provided it holds at least the vault's proposal threshold of voting-eligible stake and is not inside its proposal cooldown. Vote with exactly the weight its own stake carries, no more than any member holding the same stake.
Cannot
Execute a rebalance the members did not pass. Pause the vault. Upgrade the contracts. Reprice shares. Move, freeze or seize another member's funds. Skip the timelock. Receive the exit fee as a payment. The fee fraction stays in the vault and is never routed to the operator identity, though it reaches the operator's own shares like every other holder's. The economics section below sets that out.
Is
Attested by the registry at vault creation, and immutable for that vault. There is no rebind. The payout address is permanent, which is the whole argument for making it a multisig rather than an EOA.
There is no key rotation

A compromised operator identity cannot be rotated, replaced or revoked. The only remedy is to wind the vault down through exits and launch a new one. Choose the payout address as if you can never change it, because you cannot.

Economics

The fee is small by design, and we will not project it for you.

What is earned
10% of realized profit, crystallized when a member redeems. Never accrued on paper gains. A high-water mark is kept per member and per operator, and it follows that operator identity across vaults instead of resetting with each new one. If a member realized a loss under you, you earn no performance fee from that member unless and until that loss is recovered, which may never happen. Nothing stops an operator abandoning a loss-carrying identity and registering a fresh one, which resets the mark. The only cost is a visibly empty track record on the leaderboard. The enforcement here is reputation, not code.
What is not routed to you
No management fee. No deposit fee. No spread. Nothing accrues while a member simply holds. The exit fee is never routed to the operator identity, but you are also a member, and the fee stays in the vault where it raises the value of every share. An operator holding the 5% the creator gate requires collects at least 5% of every exit fee, exactly as any other holder of that stake would.
What that adds up to
10% of realized profit on a vault at the planned 50,000 USDC cap is not a salary, and this page is not going to model it, illustrate it, or put a number on it. If you are here for the fee, the arithmetic will disappoint you before the code does.
What is actually on offer
A public, attributable, on-chain record of your decisions. Every proposal you opened, every vote you cast and every execution that followed is permanently attached to an identity that cannot be rotated away from a bad quarter. That record is the point. It is worth exactly as much as your decisions turn out to be worth, and no more.
