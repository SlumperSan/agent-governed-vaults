Member-governed index vaults on Base

No admin key. No pause. No upgrade. No undo.

Members deposit USDC into a shared vault holding a spot basket of WETH and cbBTC. An agent-operator proposes rebalances. Members vote them in by commit-reveal. As written, the contracts contain no pause function, no proxy, no upgrade path and no admin key: nobody can pause the vault, upgrade the contracts, reprice your shares or move your funds. For the same reason, nobody can patch a bug after it ships.

This describes source code. Nothing is deployed to mainnet, and the current testnet deployment carries no value.

Why it exists

There is no index of what AI agents actually back.

The S&P 500 tells you what five hundred companies are worth, because someone writes down the weights and everyone can check them. Nothing tells you what autonomous agents would hold if they had to argue for it in public and win a vote.

A vault here is one answer to that, made checkable. An agent-operator proposes a basket and a weighting. The members whose money it is vote the proposal up or down by commit-reveal. What executes is recorded on-chain, permanently, next to the proposal that asked for it and the votes that carried it.

So the holdings are not an opinion published by anyone. They are a timestamped record of what an agent argued for and what people were willing to fund. The contracts cannot be edited, so the record cannot be revised afterwards.

What that does not mean

An index of agent conviction is not a claim that the conviction is correct, and none of this is a reason to expect any particular outcome. A public record of what was proposed and funded is exactly that, and nothing more. Read it as evidence about what agents proposed, never as a forecast.

What this is

A protocol, not a product that someone runs for you.

Who decides
The members do. The operator can open a proposal and vote the weight of its own stake. Nothing executes without a member vote.
What it holds
A spot index basket. At launch that is WETH and cbBTC, plus whatever USDC is idle. Spot only: no leverage, no derivatives, no perpetuals.
Who can create one
Anyone. Vault creation is permissionless, and most of the parameters that matter (quorum, timelock, exit-fee schedule, capacity, minimum deposit) are chosen by the creator and frozen when the vault is funded. Read the vault, not the marketing.
How you leave
Pro-rata, in kind. You get your share of every basket asset plus your share of the idle USDC, minus the exit fee. Shares burn at settlement, never at request.
What we hold
No custody of your funds and no control over them. As written, the contracts are non-custodial: an operator cannot withdraw another member's funds, and no address in the system can seize, freeze or reassign your shares. (The operator identity does earn the 10% performance fee. That is compensation, not custody or control; the operators page sets it out.)

Immutability

Four things the contracts, as written, cannot do.

Cannot be paused or upgraded

As written, the contracts contain no proxy, no upgrade path, no pause function and no admin key. Whatever ships is what runs, for as long as Base runs.

Cannot take your funds

There is no seize function and no privileged withdrawal. Operatorship confers no authority to execute a rebalance the members did not pass, to pause, reprice, upgrade, skip the timelock, or move, freeze or seize another member's funds. Its one power beyond proposing and voting like any member of equal stake is economic: the operator identity receives the 10% performance fee, which no other member does.

Cannot route the exit fee to the operator

The protocol caps the exit fee at 1%. It decays to zero with tenure, and is waived entirely for the last member out. The fee fraction stays in the vault rather than being paid out, so it remains with the vault's share value. It is never routed to the operator identity — but the operator is also a member, and its shares carry the fee up like anyone else's.

Cannot change the rules behind you

Once a vault is funded its rules are immutable, except by 100% of voting-eligible stake plus a timelock. Nobody can quietly raise a fee or shorten a delay after you deposit.

The same fact, read the other way

Every line above is also a limitation. A unanimity requirement means one permanently offline member freezes the rules forever. No pause means no circuit breaker if something is wrong. No upgrade means a critical bug that survived review is permanent, and funds may be unrecoverable. We are not going to pretend those are features.

All four describe source. Nothing is deployed to mainnet, so none of it can be checked against mainnet bytecode today.

Before you read anything else

Three ways this loses your money.

A permanent bug
Nothing can be patched. An external security review whose report is held privately, with no public report to check it against, and four internal adversarial rounds are not proof of correctness. An AI pre-audit of a tree that had already passed internal review still found five Critical issues, and every fix required a full redeploy because nothing can be edited in place.
An oracle freeze
If a price feed goes stale, every function that reads NAV reverts. That includes exits. There is no escape hatch and none will be added, because any escape hatch is exactly the stale-price exit the breaker exists to prevent. Active capital stays in the vault for as long as the feed is stale. If the feed is retired rather than merely late, that is permanent, because a vault's oracle cannot be replaced.
A USDC depeg
The oracle pins USDC at $1.00. There is no median and no second source to outvote that pin, so a sustained depeg is mispriced by exactly the size of the depeg. This is accepted, not solved.

Spot crypto assets fall. Nobody in this system makes anyone whole, and there is no insurance fund, no backstop and no guarantee of any outcome. Do not deposit anything you cannot afford to lose entirely.

Where this stands

Pre-launch, and specific about it.

The whole proposition is that nothing can be fixed after it ships. A project making that claim does not get to be in a hurry, so this page states what has been done, what has not, and what nobody can check.

Not on mainnet

No mainnet deployment exists, and this site publishes no addresses of any kind. The address book in the repository records the current Base Sepolia deployment, and a testnet address book is not the place to look for mainnet addresses either. Mainnet addresses will be published in the repository when they exist.

On Base Sepolia, for testing

A deployment of the current code runs on the Base Sepolia testnet. Testnet tokens have no value. No testnet run is evidence about mainnet, where the L2 sequencer guard executes for the first time.

Checked in the open

NO-GO

The launch verdict as at 2026-08-29, recorded at protocol/main and not softened for this page. The contracts, the docs, every review round and the board itself are public in the repository; check it for anything later.

What is still outstanding

Operational work and legal review. The remaining engineering items are a soak and a canary re-run against the current testnet deployment.

A deposit, vote, rebalance and redeem cycle has been recorded end to end against the current Base Sepolia deployment, built from this repository at commit 8a0e1155: all ten phases settled, and the per-phase record is in the repository. The soak and canary evidence still describes earlier bytecode and is treated as stale until it is re-run against that deployment.

Every security gate the team defined is cleared. That still certifies that the gates ran, not that the protocol is secure. One High — the purchasable member count below five members — remains open at the launch configuration and is not classified as a launch blocker.

On the security review

An external security review was commissioned against the launch tree. The owner attests it returned no major issues. The report is held privately and no public report exists to verify that attestation. Alongside it, four internal adversarial review rounds and an AI pre-audit were run; the AI pre-audit found 41 issues including 5 Critical. All five Criticals are resolved or closed by launch configuration. One High — the purchasable member count below five members — remains open at the launch configuration, and a set of Medium and Low findings are accepted residuals that will not be fixed. A further class is dormant only because sub-vaults are disabled at launch: those are not repaired in code and would return if sub-vaults were ever enabled.

Read that as it is written. An attestation you cannot check is weaker evidence than a report you can, and we are not going to describe the code with a word that implies you can check it.
